Designing a modular, 3D-printable ROV

How a finite printer envelope shaped Stingray’s wing and chassis architecture.

Blog / Entry

The design constraint

Stingray is a stingray-inspired underwater robot concept. The first prototype is intended to be printable on my Bambu Lab A1, which has a 256 × 256 × 256 mm build volume. Full-size body and wing geometry must therefore be split into manageable segments.

Designing for assembly

The mechanical plan uses built-in attachment features to avoid post-print drilling, separate upper and lower body sections, and dedicated space for electronics. A previously generated parts manifest records candidate pieces inside the printer envelope. A bounding-box check is not evidence of slicer fit, joint strength or assembly accuracy.

What comes next

I will measure representative joints and verify fit before committing to full-size assemblies. Mechanical design, buoyancy, sealing and structural verification remain separate milestones.
