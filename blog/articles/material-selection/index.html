PETG, PETG-CF and the reality of prototyping

Material selection is a trade-off between repeatability, print quality and eventual performance.

Blog / Entry

A practical baseline

I initially explored carbon-fiber-filled PETG, using a 0.6 mm hardened-steel nozzle as the current printer baseline. Earlier experiments with PETG-CF produced stringing and quality issues. Regular PETG gave better qualitative results and became the practical prototype material.

A new failure mode

The November wing-print failures were in regular PETG. The photographs show defects concentrated around supported features; the cause is still under investigation. This matters because changing filament alone does not establish a reliable manufacturing process.

A measured approach

Future trials will preserve the slicer profile, orientation, filament, support geometry, print time, waste and dimensions. Small coupons can isolate variables before repeating a much larger print. No material has yet been validated for the intended underwater application.
